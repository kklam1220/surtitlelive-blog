# Blog 23 localization brief — SurtitleLive → Pockitle Cue

English source: `blog/src/content/blog/23-surtitlelive-becomes-pockitle-cue.md`

## Editorial intent

This is a product-name transition article, not a launch-hype announcement. Preserve the causal structure: real use since June, more than 200 caption deployments, Pockitle Live in August, and the practical constraints that led to Pockitle Cue for Mac and Pockitle Live for iOS.

## Product truth to preserve

- `Pockitle Cue` is the new name for SurtitleLive; existing accounts and projects continue.
- `Pockitle Cue for Mac` and `Pockitle Live for iOS` are still in development. Do not present them as generally available.
- Browser Pockitle Cue already supports continuity for already-open local projection through a venue network interruption. The Mac app is about a clearer local show-time environment, synced local data, display handling, and QLab integration — not inventing offline projection from zero.
- In Pockitle Cue for Mac, prepared surtitles can be selected in the app and dragged directly into a QLab cue list.
- Pockitle Live is for speech generated in the moment, not a replacement for reviewed prepared surtitles.
- On Android, compatible browser/device combinations can accept external audio input. In the currently supported iOS browser workflow, operators cannot rely on external microphone or venue-mixer selection; the dependable browser capture path is the device microphone.
- Pockitle Live for iOS is being developed to use native iOS audio paths, including supported USB audio interfaces and venue mixer feeds. The app is for operators; audience members still use the browser.

## Terminology

Follow `docs/guides/ops/translation-review-runbook.md` and the existing locale catalogs. Keep protected product/platform names unchanged: Pockitle Cue, Pockitle Cue for Mac, Pockitle Live, Pockitle Live for iOS, QLab, Android, iOS, iPhone, iPad, Mac, USB and LED.

Translate ordinary prose and technical nouns whenever the locale has a natural established equivalent. In particular, do not leave English residue such as `prepared surtitling`, `live captioning`, `show`, `workflow`, `interface`, `mixer`, `browser`, `deployment`, `output`, or `Internet` merely because the English source uses it. Standard local loanwords remain acceptable when they are genuinely the normal native term.

`cue` is theatre vocabulary and may remain where that locale normally uses it, especially in QLab context; otherwise use the locale's established theatre term from the runbook. Preserve the distinction between theatre translation surtitles and accessibility captions.
